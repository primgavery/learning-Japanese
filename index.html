    Achievements

    Kana chart (ひらがな・カタカナ)

    Every reading in this app is spelled out with these. Worth learning before anything else — once you can read hiragana and katakana, everything else here gets a lot easier. Practice them in the Quiz tab's Kana mode.

    Number building blocks

    Japanese numbers are built compositionally. Learn these pieces and you can read almost any number.

    #ReadingHiragana

    Sound changes to memorize: 100 = ひゃく (not いちひゃく), 300 = さんびゃく (not さんひゃく), 600 = ろっぴゃく, 800 = はっぴゃく, 1000 = せん (not いっせん), 3000 = さんぜん, 8000 = はっせん.

    Counters (助数詞)

    Japanese uses different counting words depending on what you're counting — this is the part that trips up Spanish/English speakers, since neither language does this. Cards are sorted by how often you'll actually need them — つ and 人 are the two to overlearn first, since between "generic object" and "person" they cover most everyday counting. Each card shows the full 1–10 pattern and the sound-change rule behind it.

    Telling time (何時何分)

    Hours have their own irregulars: 4時 = よじ (not yonji), 7時 = しちじ (not nanaji), 9時 = くじ (not kyuuji). Minutes reuse the 分 pattern above. For :30, はん (han, "half") is more natural than saying さんじゅっぷん.
